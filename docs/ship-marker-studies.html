Fleet Marker Studies

Seven Windstar hulls on a night globe, currently drawn as a 22 px silhouette with a near-black outline — the same value as the water behind it. Three replacements were drafted, each built from a static base, a heading-rotated hull, and a ring that pulses off the ship’s position. B shipped. A and C were stripped from the codebase; this page is the record of what they looked like and why they lost.

Where it stands

The problem isn’t the shape, it’s the mass and the contrast. At globe zoom the hull is a few pixels of green, and its dark outline disappears into open water — so a ship reads as a speck until you already know where to look.

Three studies

Each stage below is live: the marker at regional zoom on the left, and the whole seven-ship fleet at globe zoom (0.78×) crossing from open ocean onto a lit coastline — the case where a dark outline fails today.

Decision · shipped

B — Sonar Contact

It buys the most legibility per pixel. The reticle is the only one of the three built from straight lines and right angles, and a map of coastlines, weather cells and storm cones has almost none of those — so the eye finds it immediately, over water and over land alike. The brackets also stay upright while the hull rotates inside them, which keeps heading readable instead of turning the whole marker into a spinning blob.

It is also already the console’s own language. The screensaver fires a cyan sonar shockwave when it orbits a ship; making that the resting state of every contact means the globe and the screensaver finally say the same thing. Three rings on a 2 s loop reads as an active sweep rather than a slow throb.

Shipped since: B and C were never exclusive, and C’s always-visible name label now hangs under B’s reticle on the globe — a Cesium label on the ship entity, toggleable from the sidebar. It is the single biggest win for finding a specific ship, since seven labels never crowd a world view.

B is what the globe draws now. Its artwork and timings live in client/src/layers/ships/shipMarkers.ts; the reticle, hull and rings are assembled as three stacked billboards in ShipLayer.tsx. A and C survive only in this page.

Rings freeze mid-expansion — keeping the extra presence, dropping the motion — for anyone with prefers-reduced-motion set, and the render pump idles whenever no ship is on screen.
